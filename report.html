The Craps Strategy Ledger

Eighteen betting systems, simulated against a validated dice engine. The ranking never changes: the house edge decides everything, and no arrangement of bets escapes it.

The finding. Every strategy loses money at a rate set by one number — the house edge on the bets it uses. Systems don't change that number; they only change how fast you meet it and how violently your bankroll swings on the way. The only real lever is free odds, the single bet on the table that pays true.

Odds dilute the edge — they don't beat it

Adding free odds lowers the edge on your total action because the odds portion is a fair bet. The flat-bet loss is unchanged; it's just spread over more money. That's why the curve flattens rather than crossing zero.

The catch: bigger odds mean bigger swings. At 5x the standard deviation is $351 a session; at 10x, sessions start busting the $1,000 bankroll outright. A lower edge on a wilder ride is a real trade, not a free win.

Three systems that fool people

The Iron Cross — wins almost every roll, loses fastest but one

Field plus place 5, 6 and 8 covers every number except the 7. It wins on about five rolls out of six, which feels unbeatable. It bleeds 3.79% — the 7 takes three bets at once, and it arrives more often than any other number.

The Martingale — a 76% win rate that still loses

Doubling after losses produced a +$150 median: most sessions genuinely finish ahead. It also busted the full bankroll 12.3% of the time, and the mean is −$16. Many small wins funding rare total losses is not an edge — it's the same loss reshaped.

The hedge — paying to protect a bet

Backing the pass line with any-craps raises the edge from 1.31% to 1.44%. The hedge is a worse bet than the one it protects, so buying insurance from an 11% bet to guard a 1.4% bet costs more than the risk removed.

If you want to play

Bet the pass line or don't pass, take the largest free odds your bankroll tolerates, and add place bets on the 6 and 8 if you want more action. Ignore the field, the hardways, and every proposition in the middle of the table. That's the whole optimal strategy — the rest of the layout exists to sell you a worse number.

Simulated with a custom engine validated against closed-form probability: every bet's measured edge matches its exact value (pass 1.41%, don't pass 1.36%, place 6/8 1.52%, field 5.56%, any 7 16.67%). Validation caught four engine bugs — come bets paying on arrival, come-out sevens taking bets that were off, and two errors in how action was counted — each of which had produced impossible results, including a strategy that appeared to beat the house.
